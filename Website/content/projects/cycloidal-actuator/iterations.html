1. Mechanical improvement

    Redesign the output-shaft structure and refine gear-profile clearance

    Improving the output-shaft structure and adjusting gear-profile clearance made the actuator more efficient and easier to back-drive. I carried those changes into later prototypes while continuing to refine the fits and assembly.

  2. Packaging revisions

    Make room for the electronics and the assembly process

    The package had to accommodate the controller board, bearings, output flange, and motor. Our project review also identified heat near printed parts, airflow, wiring routes, and fastener access as areas for further work.

  3. Documented reducer variants

    Compare the 17:1 and 25:1 designs

    The repository retains the original 17:1 design, which is no longer maintained, alongside the revised 25:1 assembly. The newer version uses a different output-shaft sleeve bearing and addresses torque margin. The output-shaft and clearance revisions addressed the mechanical losses.

Reduction also changes reflected inertia
Jmotor → output = G²Jmotor
Jload → motor = Jload / G²

These ideal relations describe how reduction affects the inertia seen at each side of the actuator. I consider that effect alongside torque margin and back-driving resistance.
