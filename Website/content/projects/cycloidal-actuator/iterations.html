1. Mechanical improvement

    Redesign the output-shaft structure and refine gear-profile clearance

    The main improvement in efficiency and back-driving came from a better output-shaft structure and revised gear-profile clearance. I incorporated those changes into later prototypes, alongside continued work on fit, mechanical support, and assembly.

  2. Packaging revisions

    Make room for the electronics and the assembly process

    The CAD evolved around the controller board, bearings, output flange, and motor package. The project review also identified heat near printed material, airflow, wiring routes, and fastener access.

  3. Documented reducer variants

    Compare the 17:1 and 25:1 designs

    The original 17:1 design was retained in the repository but is no longer maintained. The revised 25:1 assembly changes the reduction and output-shaft sleeve bearing. The reduction-ratio revision addressed torque margin. The output-shaft structure and gear-profile clearance were the main changes behind the efficiency and back-driving improvement.

Reduction also changes reflected inertia
Jmotor → output = G²Jmotor
Jload → motor = Jload / G²

These ideal relations help explain why torque multiplication, responsiveness, and back-driving need to be considered together. A higher ratio is not a universal improvement.
