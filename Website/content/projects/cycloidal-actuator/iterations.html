1. Prototype loop

    Revisit fit and mechanical support

    I revisited clearances, bearing selection and support, print orientation, and mechanical interfaces. The assembly review also flagged tight fits and binding risk. These were practical candidates to investigate, rather than proof of a single confirmed loss mechanism.

  2. Packaging revisions

    Make room for the electronics and the assembly process

    The CAD evolved around the controller board, bearings, output flange, and motor package. The project review also identified heat near printed material, airflow, wiring routes, and fastener access.

  3. Documented reducer variants

    Compare the 17:1 and 25:1 designs

    The original 17:1 design was retained in the repository but is no longer maintained. The revised 25:1 assembly changes the reduction and output-shaft sleeve bearing. This revision targets torque margin; it is not, by itself, evidence that back-driving or efficiency improved.

Reduction also changes reflected inertia
Jmotor → output = G²Jmotor
Jload → motor = Jload / G²

These ideal relations help explain why torque multiplication, responsiveness, and back-driving need to be considered together. A higher ratio is not a universal improvement.
